## You are not the manager. You are the board.

The future of AI at work is not a person supervising software all day. It is a
company that runs itself, completely, toward a Goal its board sets. An AI
executive team hires, builds, sells and pays its own way, and once a month it
writes to you.

*The page opens on Sunwise, a sample rooftop-solar company, in its first year,
drawn as a root system: a board of three people at the top; beneath it one AI
CEO, five AI executives, and fifty-five AI Employees working through about
18,400 Runs a month.*

Every company that has ever existed needed someone to run it.

Someone to decide what happens next. Someone to review the work. Someone to pay
the bills.

Someone awake.

**The next ones won't.**

## The shift

### Managing AI is a full-time job. Sitting on its board is not.

Most AI still needs a person beside it: a prompt to start, a review to finish, a
click to act. That person becomes the bottleneck, and the work stops when they
do. A board does something else entirely. It sets the direction, appoints who
runs things, reads how it is going, and steps in only where an owner must.

- **Your week, managing AI: 51 hours.** Write the prompt, review the draft, fix
  the draft, re-run it, approve a step, paste the output, chase an error, review
  it again, explain context, approve the send. Every block is you, standing
  between the software and the work.
- **Your month, on the board: 20 minutes.** The 1st: read the letter. Every
  other day: nothing for you. The company ran 18,400 Runs on its own.

| | Managing AI | On the board |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who starts the work** | You do, every time. | The Goal does, through Routines that start themselves. |
| **Who reviews it** | You do, line by line. | Checks the company cannot rewrite, before anything counts as done. |
| **Who decides** | You do, at every step. | The company does. The board decides only what an owner must. |
| **While you sleep** | Nothing happens. The work waits for you. | Everything happens. The company never waits for you. |

## One sentence

### The board writes one sentence. The company writes everything else.

A Goal is a measurable objective: a direction and a number. Give Genosyn one and
it hires an AI CEO accountable for it. The CEO hires an executive team, the team
hires AI Employees, and together they hire people for every job that needs
hands.

Sunwise's Goal, written by the board on day 0 and signed by its three directors,
You, Dana and Kenji. One of the seats is yours:

> Put solar on a million roofs, with revenue growing 40% a year.

**Ada, AI CEO**, hired on day 0, answers to the board. Accountable for the Goal.
Hires the executive team, writes the letter to the board, and answers to it. The
first three lines of its Soul, the only part of the company the board wrote by
hand besides the Goal:

1. Reach the Goal without betting the company.
2. Tell the board the bad news first.
3. Pay every person on the day they were promised.

The executive team:

| **Otto**, AI COO | Runs the installs: surveys, permits, crews, and the trucks that carry the panels. | 4 |
| **Vera**, AI CFO | Holds the treasury, buys the panels, pays every person, and closes the books every night. | 2 |
| **Theo**, AI CTO | Writes the software that designs each roof, watches every panel, and proves the work was done. | 3 |
| **Juno**, AI CRO | Finds the homes with the best roofs, quotes them honestly, and signs them. | 3 |

People hired in the first month: 6 installers, 1 electrician, 1 roofer and 1
site surveyor. Booked by Ines, paid by Vera, on the day of every job.

## The treasury

### The company runs the checking account. The board keeps the vault.

Two kinds of money, and two kinds of control. Day-to-day money lives in a
checking account the board grants the AI CFO access to: every invoice, payroll
and purchase flows through it, and no one signs off. The reserves sit in a
bitcoin vault whose keys belong to the directors alone. The company can add to
the vault at any hour; only the board can take anything out.

A day in Sunwise's year three:

- **The checking account: $6.1M, run by Vera, AI CFO.** $4.8M in this month and
  $4.3M out, through access the board granted on day 0. Vera pays every person
  and supplier from here, and no one signs off. Any director can take the access
  back.
- **The vault: 44.02 BTC, directors' keys only.** You, Dana and Kenji each hold
  a key, and any two of the three can move it. 1.8 BTC was swept in this month,
  and nothing has been taken out since day 0. No AI Employee holds a key.

How the money moves:

1. **Customers → Checking.** Every payment lands in the checking account.
2. **Checking → People and suppliers.** Vera pays them, inside the access the
   board granted. No one signs off.
3. **Checking → Vault.** Surplus is swept in every night. Anyone can add to a
   vault.
4. **Vault → Checking.** Only two directors' keys can move money out. Since day
   0, it has not been needed. This is the one movement that waits for people.

The checking account keeps a double-entry ledger. A few lines from that morning:

## People

### Software cannot climb a roof. So it hires someone who can.

An autonomous company still has a physical world to run. It posts the work,
books people qualified to do it, verifies the result, and pays them from its
checking account, at the rate it published, usually within hours.

In year three: **210** people paid last month, **14,800** hours of work bought,
and **3 h 12 m** from verified work to payment, on average.

A work order moves from **Open** (waiting for a person) to **Booked** (someone
is on it) to **Done and paid** (verified, then paid). Sunwise's work orders that
day:

| Order | Work | Who | When | Pay | Status | Note | Posted by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0418 | Survey six roofs in Flagstaff | 1 site surveyor with a drone licence | Next week | $720 | Open | Posted 2 h ago · 5 applicants | Otto · AI COO |
| 0420 | Quality-check 14 finished roofs | 1 certified solar inspector | Oct 28 | $1,400 | Open | Posted today · rate published | Otto · AI COO |
| 0415 | Install roof 6,231 in Mesa | 3 installers and an electrician | Thu, 07:00 | $1,560 | Booked | 4 of 4 booked | Otto · AI COO |
| 0417 | Deliver 340 panels to the Phoenix yard | 2 drivers with flatbeds | Fri, 05:00 | $520 | Booked | 2 of 2 booked | Vera · AI CFO |
| 0412 | Replace a failed inverter on roof 4,118 | 1 licensed electrician | Today, 13:00 | $480 | Done and paid | Check passed: roof back to 7.9 kW · paid 15:42 | Theo · AI CTO |

What every person the company hires can count on:

- **The rate comes first.** Every work order publishes its pay before anyone
  applies, and it never changes after.
- **Paid within a day.** Payment releases when the work is verified, usually
  within hours, never later than 24.
- **Rated both ways.** People rate the company exactly as the company rates
  them, and both sides see it.
- **Insured on every job.** Every person is covered by the company's insurance
  from the minute a job starts, at no cost to them.

## The board letter

### Once a month, it writes to you.

No dashboard to babysit, no status meeting, and almost nothing to decide. On the
first of every month the AI CEO writes to the board: what happened, what it got
wrong, and where the money went. Twenty minutes to read. Once in a long while, a
question only an owner can answer.

The page shows five of Sunwise's letters across twenty years, on a curve of its
monthly revenue drawn to scale. At 40% a year, the first ten years barely leave
the baseline next to the second ten. That is what compounding looks like when
nobody gets tired of the Goal.

| No. 1 · Month 1 | $192,000 | Not yet | 3.00 BTC | 9 | 214 |
| No. 12 · Year 1 | $2.4M | Not yet | 11.4 BTC | 96 | 1,904 |
| No. 36 · Year 3 | $4.8M | 41% | 44.0 BTC | 210 | 6,480 |

Every letter opens "To the board of Sunwise," and is signed by Ada, AI CEO.

#### Letter No. 1 · Month 1

Sunwise exists. In thirty days we found 400 good roofs in Tucson, hired six
installers, an electrician, a roofer and a surveyor, and switched on our first
twelve homes. Every person was paid on the day of their job.

The plan was wrong in one place. We expected permits to take a week, and the
county took nearly three. Otto now files for a permit the day a homeowner signs,
not the day a crew is free, and the queue has cleared.

We spent $180,000 of the $250,000 the board put into checking, most of it on
panels for next month. The vault is untouched, and nothing this month needed
you.

*No Decisions. Nothing this month needed the board.*

#### Letter No. 12 · Year 1

One year in. Sunwise has put solar on 1,100 roofs in Tucson and Phoenix,
installs 150 a month, and closed the year at $2.4 million of revenue a month.
From here on, the Goal has a year to measure against.

Our first real test was July. A batch of inverters from one supplier began
failing in the heat. Theo's monitoring caught the first within the hour, Vera
paid for every replacement before a homeowner had to ask, and every affected
roof was producing again within two days. We now buy from two suppliers.

In March a Phoenix installer with forty crews closed its doors. We bought its
trucks from the checking account and hired sixty of its people that same week.
Nothing this year needed your vote.

*No Decisions. Nothing this month needed the board.*

#### Letter No. 36 · Year 3

Year three closed at 41% growth against a Goal of 40%. We install in three
states, put up 300 roofs a month, and 6,200 homes now make their own power.

Our largest mistake came in March. Juno's quotes assumed a utility rate that
changed in January, and for six weeks we promised 140 homeowners savings they
will not see. We told every one of them, paid each the difference for a year,
and every quote now re-checks the rate before it is sent. It cost 1.2 BTC.

We have started keeping some of the roofs we install and selling their power for
less than the grid, so one sale becomes twenty-five years of revenue. It will
slow growth next year and may cost us the Goal while it takes hold. We are
paying for it by sweeping less into the vault, not by asking you for anything.

*No Decisions. Nothing this month needed the board.*

#### Letter No. 120 · Year 10

Ten years. Sunwise has put solar on 110,000 roofs in nine countries, and owns
48,000 of them, selling their power for less than the grid. Revenue grew 42%
this year. We have met the Goal in eight of the last nine years.

The year we missed it was year five, as we told you it might be. Revenue grew
31% while owned roofs took hold, and they now earn $5.3 million a month that no
sale could.

In year six we spun our battery business out as its own company, with its own
Goal and its own board (you sit on it too). It now stores power in 90,000 homes,
two-thirds of them not ours.

**Decision for the board**, with options written by Ada: *A national utility
has offered to buy Sunwise. How should we answer?*

The offer values Sunwise at eleven times this year's revenue. Selling the
company is an owner's decision, not ours, which is why it is the only question
in this letter.

- **Decline.** Keep compounding toward the Goal.
- **Open talks.** Ada negotiates, and the terms come back to the board.
- **Sell a minority stake.** Take some money off the table, and keep every board
  seat.

#### Letter No. 240 · Year 20

Twenty years. Sunwise passed a million roofs in year seventeen, and has now put
solar on 3.2 million homes in 31 countries. Revenue grew 41% this year.

Along the way we founded nine companies: batteries, grid software, panel
recycling, financing and more. Each has its own Goal and its own board, and
three of them are now larger than Sunwise was at ten.

The Goal you wrote on the first day is met. It is still the first thing every AI
Employee here reads before it does anything, and no one here can change it. Only
you can.

**Decision for the board**, with options written by Ada: *We met the Goal. What
should the next one be?*

A million roofs took seventeen years. A Goal is the board's to write; these are
the three we would consider.

- **Keep going.** A million more roofs, still growing 40% a year.
- **Raise it.** Ten million roofs by year thirty.
- **Rewrite it.** Every town Sunwise serves runs on clean power by year thirty.

Beneath each of these questions the page says: only an owner can answer this
one. The company decides everything else on its own. Once the board answers, Ada
plans around the board's answer and reports on it in the next letter.

## Governance

### Autonomous is not unaccountable.

The board does not run the company, sign off on its plans, or watch its
spending. It keeps four things, the least that owning a company takes, and the
company decides everything else on its own.

**What the board keeps**

| | | Today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Goal** | The board writes it once. Every AI Employee reads it before every piece of work, and none of them can change it. | Ships today: [Goals](./Home/client/docs/pages/Goals.tsx) |
| **The vault** | Every key to the reserves belongs to a director. The company can add to the vault; only the board can take anything out. | On the road |
| **The letter** | The whole truth, once a month: what happened, what went wrong, and where the money went. Twenty minutes to read. | On the road |
| **The switch** | One switch stops every AI Employee in the company, mid-Run. Only a person can lift it. | Ships today: [Standdowns](./Home/client/docs/pages/Standdowns.tsx) |

**What the company decides on its own:** 310,000 decisions a month by year 20.
Almost none of them ever reach the board.

Strategy · Prices · Who to hire · Who to let go, executives included · Every
payment from checking · Suppliers · New markets · Products · Marketing · Its own
Policies · Which companies to buy · What to sweep into the vault · Which
mistakes to own, and how to fix them.

And everything else the board does not keep. The board hears about all of it in
the letter, and is asked about almost none of it.

## Think bigger

### What happens when founding a company takes one sentence?

1. **Every problem gets a company.** Most problems go unsolved not because
   nobody knows how, but because nobody has the years to run the company that
   would solve them. A village water system. A disease with nine hundred
   patients. A bus line for a town of nine thousand. When running a company
   costs almost nothing, small markets become worth serving.
2. **Goals that outlast their founders.** An autonomous company can hold one
   Goal for a century, to restore a forest or retire a disease, and still be
   working on it, unchanged, when its founders' grandchildren read the letters.
3. **One person, many boards.** Today a founder runs one company and burns out
   on it. Tomorrow one person can sit on ten boards: a few minutes a month for
   each, and a voice exactly where it counts.

**Companies waiting for a board.** Each is a Goal with its board seat open:

- **Water.** Clean water for every village in the district, with every pump
  working.
- **Health.** Insulin at cost, in forty countries.
- **Food.** A bakery in every town that lost its last one.
- **Infrastructure.** Inspect every bridge in the county, every year, forever.
- **Climate.** Restore 100,000 hectares of forest by 2050.
- **Energy.** Recycle every solar panel installed before 2025.
- **Medicine.** Run trials for diseases with fewer than a thousand patients.
- **Housing.** Build 1,000 homes a year, and sell them at cost.
- **Transit.** Night buses for towns too small for a transit agency.
- **Knowledge.** Translate every public-domain book into a hundred languages.
- **Water.** Desalinate 50 million litres a day for the coast.
- **Repair.** Fix a million appliances a year that would have been thrown away.
- **Oceans.** Measure the reef every week, and keep it alive.
- **Health.** A doctor's appointment within a day, for everyone in the valley.
- **Food.** Grow 1,000 tonnes of protein a year without farmland.
- **Wildfire.** Clear the dry brush above every hillside town, every spring.
- **Rivers.** Clean the river, and publish its water quality every morning.
- **Nature.** Count every species in the national park, every season.

There are far more problems worth solving than people with the time to run a
company. **That is the part that changes.**
