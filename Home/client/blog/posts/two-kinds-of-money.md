Give a company that runs itself access to money, and you have to answer an uncomfortable question: what stops it from losing all of it? The usual answer is a person who signs off on every payment. That puts a manager back in the loop, which is exactly what an autonomous company exists to remove.

The vision answers differently. It splits money the way it splits control.

## The checking account: the company's to run

Day-to-day money lives in a checking account. The board grants the AI CFO access to it, and from then on every invoice, payroll run and purchase flows through it with no one signing off. Suppliers are paid on time. People are paid within a day of their work being verified. The company buys what it needs when it needs it.

Any director can take that access back. While it stands, the company runs its own operating money, the way a well-run business does.

## The vault: the board's to keep

The reserves sit in a vault, and every key to it belongs to a director. The company can add to the vault at any hour, and sweeps its surplus in every night. It can never take anything out. Moving money out of the vault takes directors' keys: for example, any two of three.

The board also chooses what the reserves are held in. In the vision's sample company, Sunwise, the board chose bitcoin, but the design does not depend on that choice. What matters is who holds the keys.

## Why the split works

The checking account is fast and the vault is slow, on purpose:

- **A mistake in checking is bounded.** The most the company can lose is what is in the account, and surplus keeps flowing out of reach, into the vault.
- **A disaster needs people.** Draining the reserves requires the people who own the company to agree, with their own keys.
- **Nothing everyday waits.** The ordinary work never queues behind a human signature.

It is the same principle as the rest of the vision. The company decides everything it can be trusted to decide, and the board keeps the few things an owner must.

## What the board sees

Every month, the letter says where the money went: what came in, what went out, and what was swept into the vault. In the vision, every figure traces back to the ledger, and an auditor the company cannot appoint checks the books.

The ledger part ships today: Genosyn's Finance section keeps double-entry books. The treasury, its reserve vault and the auditor are what we are building next.
