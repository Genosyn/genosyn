What does a company that runs itself actually do all day? This is one day at Sunwise, the sample rooftop-solar company the vision page follows, in its third year. None of it happened. All of it is how the vision means a company to work, and every figure matches the vision page.

## Before dawn

At 05:40 an inspector, K. Mensah, finishes three hours of inspections and is paid $270. Nobody signed anything. The work was verified, so the money moved.

At 06:05 Vera, the AI CFO, pays Sonoran Inverter Co. $36,000 for thirty inverters. Sunwise buys from two inverter suppliers now. In its first summer a batch from a single supplier failed in the heat, and the company decided never to depend on one again. That decision, and why, is in the record.

## Morning

At 06:30 a homeowner in Phoenix pays $16,400 in full for roof 6,201. At 07:02 Sunwise pays the county $4,200 in permit fees for fourteen roofs, each filed on the day its homeowner signed. Filing on signing day is another old lesson: in the first month, the plan assumed a week and the county took nearly three.

By 08:12 a site surveyor, a roofer and an electrician have all been paid for their hours. At 08:31 the nightly sweep moves 0.40 BTC of surplus into the vault, where only the directors' keys can reach it. At 08:47 Vera pays Desert Panel Supply $61,200 for 340 panels.

## Midday

The panels need moving, so work order 0417 is already booked: two drivers with flatbeds, Friday at 05:00, $520, posted by Vera. Otto, the AI COO, posts work order 0420: quality-check fourteen finished roofs, one certified solar inspector, $1,400, with the rate published before anyone applies. The Flagstaff survey he posted two hours earlier, work order 0418, already has five applicants.

At 13:00 a licensed electrician replaces a failed inverter on roof 4,118. Theo, the AI CTO, caught the failure from the panel monitoring and posted the job. At 15:42 the roof reports 7.9 kW again, the Check passes, and the electrician is paid $480.

## All day

Around two hundred decisions are made without anyone on the board hearing about them: which crew takes which roof, which supplier gets the next order, which homeowner to call back first. Each is written down with its reason, so any of them can be read later.

Across the month, 210 people are paid for 14,800 hours of work, an average of 3 hours and 12 minutes from verified work to payment.

## What the board saw

Nothing, on the day. The board, three directors with one seat that is yours, hears about it on the first of the month, in a letter: year three closed at 41% growth against a Goal of 40%, three hundred roofs a month, and one mistake owned. In March the company's quotes had assumed a utility rate that changed in January, so it told 140 homeowners, paid each the difference for a year, and made every quote recheck the rate before it is sent.

That is the whole idea. The company does the work, keeps the record, and tells its owners the truth once a month.

> No dashboard to babysit, no status meeting, and almost nothing to decide.
