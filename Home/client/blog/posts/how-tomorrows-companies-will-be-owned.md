Ask who owns a company and, for as long as there have been companies, you have really been asking who runs it, or who pays the people who do. That is about to change. When a company can run itself, owning it no longer has to come with the job of running it, and ownership can reach the people it has always skipped.

## Ownership has always come with a job

Every company that has ever existed needed someone to run it, and ownership followed that work. Founders owned what they ran. Investors owned what they paid others to run. Everyone else, from the people who did the work to the town that depended on it, was paid, served or ignored, but almost never owned any of it.

Nobody designed it that way out of spite. Running a company is the hardest and most expensive job in it, and the people who carry that cost keep the reward. A village cannot own its water company if nobody in the village has years to give to running one. The people who do a company's work rarely own a piece of it, because ownership was the pay for a different job: running the place.

## Take away the running

On Genosyn, AI Employees do the running. Routines start the work without anyone pressing a button. Checks decide whether a Run counts as done, and the employee being graded cannot write them. When a choice is genuinely beyond the company's authority, it stops and asks, as a Decision with options rather than a request for a rubber stamp.

Follow that to its end and the owner's job shrinks to what should never be automated. Our [vision](/vision) calls it the four things a board keeps: **the Goal**, which the board writes once and no AI Employee can change; **the vault**, whose every key belongs to a director; **the letter**, the whole truth once a month; and **the switch**, which stops every AI Employee or changes who leads them.

> Take the running out of ownership, and what remains takes a few minutes a month. Anything that takes a few minutes a month can be shared.

## Who gets to own

### The people who do the work

In the vision, every job a company posts pays the published rate and a share of the company on top. Sunwise, the sample company the vision page follows, sets aside a fifth of itself on its first day for the people who will work for it. Twenty years and 52,000 people later, they own that fifth, and they chose one of its directors. Ownership stops being a lottery ticket for the first ten employees and becomes something earned the way pay is earned: by doing the work.

### The places a company serves

Many problems stay unsolved because they are too small to be worth anyone's career: a water system for one district, night buses for a town of nine thousand, a clinic for one valley. When the company runs itself, the place that needs it can own it. The village writes the Goal (clean water, every pump working), holds the keys to the reserves, and reads the letter at the village hall. The company answers to the people it serves because they are its board.

### One person, many boards

Today a founder owns one company and gives it everything. Tomorrow a person can sit on ten boards, a few minutes a month for each, with a voice exactly where it counts: the river they swim in, the disease in their family, the trade they learned. Ownership becomes a set of Goals you care about, not one job you burn out on.

## Ownership you can check

Wide ownership only works if owners can trust what they own without running it. A company run by people decides in hallways. A company run by AI Employees can show its work: every decision written down with its reason, every figure in the letter traced to the ledger, and the books checked by an auditor the company cannot appoint. One person can stop it mid-Run, and it cannot lift that stop itself.

Some of this ships today. Every AI Employee reads its company's [Goals](/docs/goals). A Run must pass its Routine's [Checks](/docs/verification), which the graded employee cannot write. A [Standdown](/docs/standdowns) stops work until a person lifts it. The treasury, the monthly letter and its auditor, and work orders that pay a share of the company are what we are building next, and [the road on the vision page](/vision#road) says which is which.

## What could go wrong

Cheap companies could just as easily concentrate ownership. If running a company costs almost nothing, one fund could own ten thousand of them. Software will not settle that alone; charters, laws and whoever writes the Goals will. What software can do is make the other path the easy one: a charter that sets aside a share for the people who will do the work, a seat on the board for the place a company serves, and books that any keyholder can audit.

It is also why Genosyn is [open source](https://github.com/Genosyn/genosyn), and why a company on it is plain text: its Goals, its Souls, its Skills, its Routines and its Policies. A company you can read is one you can own without taking anyone's word for what it does. A company you can copy is one a second town can have, under a board of its own.

## The reward changes

Ownership used to be the reward for running a company. Tomorrow it can be the reward for writing a good Goal, for doing good work, or for living somewhere a company serves. That is a different economy from the one we have, and it starts with one small change: running the company stops being anyone's job.
