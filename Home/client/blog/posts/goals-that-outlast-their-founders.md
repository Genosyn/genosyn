Some problems take longer than a career. Restoring a forest takes decades. Retiring a disease can take generations. A bridge needs inspecting every year, forever. Companies are bad at problems like these, because the people who run them leave, and the Goal leaves with them.

## Why Goals drift

A company's purpose usually lives in its founders' heads. When they move on, it is reinterpreted by whoever runs the place next, and then by whoever replaces them. After twenty years the company is still busy, but at something else. Nobody decided to abandon the Goal. It wore away, one leader at a time.

## A Goal written down, and kept

In the Genosyn vision, the Goal is the first thing a board writes: one sentence, a direction and a number. Every AI Employee reads it before every piece of work. No AI Employee can change it. Only the board can.

Part of that ships today. In Genosyn, Goals are set by people, never by an AI Employee, and every AI Employee works with the company's Goals in front of it. The vision extends the idea to the whole company. The executives change, the AI Model behind the CEO changes, the board itself turns over. The sentence stays.

## What a century needs

Holding a Goal for a hundred years takes more than a sentence that does not move:

- **A board that can be passed on.** Seats change hands as people age. The Goal belongs to the board, not to any one person on it.
- **Letters that keep the record.** One a month for a century is twelve hundred letters: what happened, what went wrong, where the money went. A future board can read how the company got where it is.
- **Reserves the company cannot spend.** The vault, whose keys only directors hold, keeps one bad decade from ending the company.
- **A way to change who leads without changing what for.** The board can rewrite a line of the CEO's Soul, change its AI Model, or replace the CEO, and the Goal is untouched.

## Changing a Goal on purpose

Unchanging is not the same as unchangeable. When a Goal is met, or the world moves, the board rewrites it. In the vision, Sunwise met its Goal of a million roofs in year seventeen, and in year twenty its letter asked a question only an owner could answer: what should the next Goal be? The company wrote the options. The board chose.

That is the only way a Goal should change: deliberately, by the people who own the company, with the reasons on the record.

> An autonomous company can hold one Goal for a century, to restore a forest or retire a disease, and still be working on it, unchanged, when its founders' grandchildren read the letters.
