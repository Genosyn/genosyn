Software got better the day it became normal to copy a good project, change it, and run your own. Companies have never worked that way. A great company in one town teaches the next town almost nothing, because what made it great lives in people's heads and habits.

## A company as plain text

On Genosyn, much of what makes a company what it is already lives in text anyone with access can read:

- **A Soul** for each AI Employee: one markdown document saying how it works and the lines it will not cross.
- **Skills**: markdown playbooks for how a piece of work is done.
- **Routines**: what runs, when, and the brief it runs from.
- **Policies**: the company-wide rules every AI Employee is bound by.
- **Goals**: what the company is for, as a sentence and a number.

Put those together and you have most of a company's operating knowledge, in a form you can read, review and copy.

## Forking a company

In the vision, that becomes a new way to start one. The best water company in one district is copied to the next under a board of its own: the same Souls, Skills and Routines, a new Goal for the new district, and new directors holding the keys. The copy starts with everything the original learned, on its first day.

That changes how good ideas spread across the map. Instead of every town reinventing its own water company, or waiting for one large company to decide it is worth the bother, a good one is copied wherever it is needed, and each copy answers to the place it serves.

## Lessons that travel

Copying is only half of it. Genosyn already learns from its own mistakes: when a Run fails, a short review writes a Lesson, its cause and advice for next time, and the next Run reads it first. An AI Employee can also propose a revision to its own Soul or a Skill based on what it learned. Nothing changes until a person applies it.

In the vision, those Lessons travel between companies. A mistake one company makes, such as quoting homeowners from a utility rate that has since changed, becomes a Lesson that spares every copy of it the same mistake. Companies learn from each other the way open-source projects do: in the open, by sharing what worked.

## Why it has to be open

You can only fork what you can read, and only trust what you can inspect. That is why Genosyn is open source, and why a company on it lives in plain text rather than inside a vendor's black box. A company you can read is one you can own without taking anyone's word for what it does.

> A company you can copy is one a second town can have, under a board of its own.
