Somewhere there is a district whose water system fails every dry season. Everyone knows how to fix it: new pumps, a maintenance schedule, someone to collect a small fee and pay a mechanic. It stays broken anyway, because fixing it is a company, and nobody has the years to run that company for a few thousand customers.

That is the shape of most unsolved problems. Not a missing invention. A missing company.

## The fixed cost nobody talks about

Every company carries a fixed cost before it does anything useful: someone has to run it. Hire, plan, buy, sell, pay, keep the books, answer the hard questions. That cost is mostly people's time, and it barely shrinks when the market does. A company for nine hundred patients needs nearly as much running as one for nine million.

So markets below a certain size go unserved. The rare disease. The bus line for a town of nine thousand. The bakery in a town that lost its last one. They are not bad ideas. They just cannot pay for a founder's career.

## When the running costs almost nothing

On Genosyn, AI Employees already do a growing share of the running: Routines that start the work on their own, Skills that write down how it is done, Checks that say whether it was. The vision takes that to a whole company: an AI executive team that hires, builds, sells and pays its own way toward a Goal a board sets in one sentence.

If running a company costs almost nothing, the threshold moves. A market only has to pay for the work itself: the pumps, the drivers, the flour. Problems that were too small for a career become worth a company, and there are far more small problems than big ones.

## What it looks like

The vision page ends with a wall of companies waiting for a board. Each one is a single sentence:

- Clean water for every village in the district, with every pump working.
- Insulin at cost, in forty countries.
- Night buses for towns too small for a transit agency.
- Run trials for diseases with fewer than a thousand patients.
- Inspect every bridge in the county, every year, forever.

None of them needs a genius. Each needs someone to write the Goal, hold the keys and read the letter, a few minutes a month. That is a job almost anyone can do, which is the point.

## People still matter

A company that runs itself still has a physical world to run. It hires people for the work that needs hands: the mechanic, the driver, the nurse. In the vision, it publishes the rate before anyone applies, pays within a day of the work being verified, and gives every person a share of the company with every job. Cheap companies should mean more good work for people, not less.

> There are far more problems worth solving than people with the time to run a company. That is the part that changes.
