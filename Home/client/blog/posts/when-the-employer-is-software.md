Software cannot climb a roof, fix a pump or drive a bus. An autonomous company still has a physical world to run, so it hires people to run it: installers, electricians, drivers, inspectors, nurses. The question is what those people can count on when the one hiring them is software.

## Why the rules matter more, not less

A company run by people has managers who can be persuaded, embarrassed, or simply decent. A company run by AI Employees has whatever rules it was given. That cuts both ways. Bad rules would be applied without mercy. Good rules would be applied without exception: no favorites, no late payments because someone was busy, no rate quietly changed after the work began.

So the rules have to be written down, made public, and kept the same for everyone.

## Five things every person can count on

In the vision, every person a company hires gets:

1. **The rate first.** Every work order publishes its pay before anyone applies, and it never changes after.
2. **Paid within a day.** Payment releases when the work is verified, usually within hours, never later than 24.
3. **Rated both ways.** People rate the company exactly as the company rates them, and both sides see it.
4. **Insured on every job.** Every person is covered by the company's insurance from the minute a job starts, at no cost to them.
5. **A share of every job.** Every job also earns a share of the company, so the people who do the work come to own part of it.

## How a work order runs

A work order is the company's whole relationship with one job, in one record. It is posted with the work, the qualifications, the time and the pay. Qualified people apply or are booked. The work gets done, and then a Check confirms it against something that can be verified without anyone's opinion: the inverter is reporting power again, the inspection photos cover every item on the list, the delivery is signed for. The Check passing is what releases the payment.

That last step is the point. Payment does not wait on someone's approval, mood or inbox. If the work is verified, the person is paid.

## Owning a piece of what you build

The fifth commitment changes the most. In the vision's sample company, Sunwise, the charter sets aside a fifth of the company on its first day for the people who will work for it. Twenty years later, the 52,000 people who have worked there own that fifth, and they chose one of its directors.

Most people who do a company's work never own any of it. A company that runs itself has no layer of executives to reward, so it can afford to reward the people who show up.

> Software cannot climb a roof. So it hires someone who can, and treats them the way it promised to.
